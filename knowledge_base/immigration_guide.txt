BARBADOS IMMIGRATION PLAIN-LANGUAGE GUIDE
A practical explanation of how Barbados immigration works, who needs what, and how to navigate the process.
This guide is a supplement to official information available at immigration.gov.bb.

---

SECTION 1: UNDERSTANDING YOUR SITUATION — WHICH CATEGORY DO YOU FALL INTO?

Before worrying about forms and fees, it helps to understand which category applies to you. Barbados immigration broadly divides people into:

VISITORS (tourists/short-stay): You are coming to Barbados for a holiday, to visit family, or for short-term business meetings. You are not working or studying. Most nationalities can enter without a visa and stay between 28 days and 6 months depending on their passport. You do not need a work permit or student visa — just a valid passport, return ticket, and sufficient funds.

REMOTE WORKERS (Welcome Stamp): You are employed by a company or clients outside of Barbados and you want to live and work in Barbados while doing your job remotely. You are not taking a Barbadian job — your income comes from outside the country. The Barbados Welcome Stamp is designed exactly for this. You apply online before or after arrival and pay a fee (US$2,000 for individuals or US$3,000 for a family). It allows you to stay and work remotely for 12 months.

EMPLOYEES (Work Permit holders): A Barbadian employer is hiring you to work in Barbados. You cannot just start working — the employer must apply for a work permit on your behalf before you begin. There are short-term permits (up to 11 months) and long-term permits (up to 3 years). The employer must also show that no Barbadian national was available to do the job (for long-term permits).

ENTREPRENEURS AND INVESTORS: You want to set up a business in Barbados. You will need a long-term work permit with proof of fund transfers and business registration. Barbados welcomes foreign investment but there is a formal process.

STUDENTS: You are coming to study at a registered educational institution in Barbados. You need a student visa, which is different from a visitor entry. Your school will typically guide you through the process. You cannot work on a student visa without separate authorization.

LONG-TERM RESIDENTS AND CITIZENS: If you want to make Barbados your permanent home, there are pathways to Immigrant Status, Permanent Resident Status, and eventually Citizenship. These are longer processes that require significant time spent in Barbados and various eligibility criteria.

SECTION 2: DO I NEED A VISA JUST TO VISIT BARBADOS?

Most visitors do NOT need a visa before arriving in Barbados. Whether you need one depends entirely on your passport.

NO VISA REQUIRED for nationals of:
- All CARICOM countries (other Caribbean nations) — stay up to 6 months
- United Kingdom — stay up to 6 months
- United States of America — stay up to 6 months
- Canada — stay up to 6 months
- All European Union member states — stay up to 3 months
- Most Commonwealth countries — stay up to 6 months
- Many other countries (Japan, Brazil, Mexico, South Korea, Switzerland, etc.) — stay 28 days to 6 months

VISA REQUIRED before travelling for nationals of:
Afghanistan, Bosnia Herzegovina, Dominican Republic, Eritrea, Haiti (regular passports), Iran, Iraq, Kosovo, Laos, Libya, Myanmar, North Korea, Papua New Guinea, Somalia, Syria, Taiwan, Yemen.

If your country requires a visa, you must apply at the nearest Barbados Embassy, High Commission, or Honorary Consul before you travel. The online visa application can also be found at immigration.gov.bb. The application fee is BDS$300 (approximately US$150) and there is a further fee if approved.

IMPORTANT: Even if you are visa-exempt, the immigration officer at the airport has the final say on entry. You must be able to show a return or onward ticket, accommodation details (hotel or host address), and sufficient funds for your stay. If you cannot, entry may be refused regardless of your nationality.

SECTION 3: ARRIVING IN BARBADOS — WHAT TO EXPECT AT THE AIRPORT

All travellers must complete the Barbados Online Immigration and Customs Form before arrival. This form becomes available 72 hours before your flight. You complete it in advance, save the receipt to your phone or print it, and show it to immigration officers when you land.

What you need ready at immigration:
- Valid passport (must be valid for the full duration of your stay; some nationalities need 6 months validity beyond travel dates)
- Completed online immigration/customs form receipt
- Return or onward ticket
- Address where you will be staying in Barbados
- Evidence of funds (cash, bank statement, or credit card — you may be asked)
- For visa-required nationalities: your entry visa

For minors (under 18) travelling without a parent:
- They must carry a notarized letter of consent from a parent or legal guardian
- The letter must state the duration of stay and name an adult who will receive the child on arrival
- Bring both the original letter and a photocopy

Entry is through Grantham Adams International Airport (Seawell, Christ Church) or Bridgetown Port for cruise passengers.

SECTION 4: THE BARBADOS WELCOME STAMP — LIVE AND WORK REMOTELY

The Barbados Welcome Stamp is a 12-month visa specifically for remote workers and digital nomads. It was one of the first such programs in the Caribbean and has been very popular.

WHO IS IT FOR?
- People who are employed by or run businesses registered outside of Barbados
- Freelancers and self-employed individuals whose clients are outside Barbados
- You must be earning income from outside the island — you cannot use this to work for a Barbadian company

WHAT IT ALLOWS:
- Legal residency in Barbados for 12 months
- The right to work remotely from Barbados
- Your family (spouse and children) can join you on the family plan

WHAT IT DOES NOT COVER:
- Working for a Barbadian employer
- Starting a Barbados-registered business to serve Barbadian clients
- Automatic renewal (you would need to apply again or transition to another status)

HOW TO APPLY:
1. Visit the official Welcome Stamp page at immigration.gov.bb or welcomestamp.bb
2. Complete the online application form
3. Pay the fee: US$2,000 for an individual OR US$3,000 for a family unit
4. Submit supporting documents:
   - Valid passport (minimum 18 months validity recommended)
   - Proof of employment or business ownership (employment letter, contract, or business registration)
   - Proof of income (recent pay slips, bank statements, or tax returns)
   - Health insurance that covers you in Barbados
   - Return ticket or proof of onward travel (some applicants provide this later)

PROCESSING: Applications are reviewed by the Immigration Department. You can apply before you arrive or after you are already in Barbados as a visitor.

FEES: US$2,000 individual / US$3,000 family. Note: Barbados uses BDS dollars locally — US$1 ≈ BDS$1.98 (the BDS dollar is pegged to the US dollar at approximately 2:1).

CONTACT: Immigration.bws@barbados.gov.bb | Tel: 535-4111, 4133, 4684, 4685

SECTION 5: WORK PERMITS — COMING TO WORK FOR A BARBADIAN EMPLOYER

If a Barbadian company wants to hire you, they must apply for a work permit on your behalf. You cannot apply yourself — the employer (called the "sponsor") is responsible.

TWO TYPES OF WORK PERMIT:

SHORT-TERM WORK PERMIT (up to 11 months):
- Used for temporary positions, contract work, or training attachments
- The employer applies using Form C-3
- Documents required:
  - Detailed cover letter from the employer
  - Form C-3 (Application for Short Term Work Permit)
  - 4 passport-sized photos
  - Copy of the bio-data (photo) page of your passport
- A training attachment follows the same process but does not carry a final fee

LONG-TERM WORK PERMIT (up to 3 years):
- Used for ongoing employment
- The employer must demonstrate that no suitable Barbadian national was available or willing to fill the role — this is the "labour market test"
- Documents required:
  - Forms C-1 and C-2 (Statement of Qualifications + Job Offer)
  - Completed Medical Form R-1, including external X-rays submitted as a report
  - 4 passport-sized photos
  - 2 character references for the applicant
  - Evidence of applicant's qualifications (degrees, certificates, professional licenses)
  - Detailed cover letter from the employer (nature of business, employment period, category, reason for the request)
  - Copy of bio-data page of passport

FEES: The application fee is BDS$300. The final fee varies depending on the category of work and duration, in accordance with the International Standard Code Fee Regulations.

HOW TO APPLY: Applications are submitted at the Immigration Department Head Office. Payments can be made online in advance through the immigration portal. Bring two copies of your payment receipt when you visit.

IMPORTANT: Do not start working before the permit is approved. Working without a permit is a violation of Barbados immigration law.

CONTACT: Immigration.workpermits@barbados.gov.bb | Tel: 535-4179, 4176, 4116, 4188, 4167, 4148

FOR ENTREPRENEURS: If you are starting a business in Barbados, you also need a work permit. Additional documents include proof of fund transfer, Certificate of Registration or Articles of Incorporation, and relevant business licences.

SECTION 6: STUDENT VISAS — COMING TO STUDY IN BARBADOS

If you are a non-national coming to study in Barbados at a recognized school, university, or training institution, you need a student visa — also called a Certificate of Eligibility for Non-Immigrant Student Status.

This is different from a visitor entry. A student visa allows you to stay in Barbados for the duration of your course.

KEY FORMS:
- Form H-1: Certificate of Eligibility for Non-Immigrant Student Status (the main application)
- Form H-2: Certificate by Non-Immigrant Student (signed declaration by the student)
- Form H-3: Notice and Report Concerning Non-Immigrant Students (used for reporting changes)
- Form H-4: Student Transfer Form (if you transfer to a different institution)

GENERAL REQUIREMENTS:
- Acceptance letter from a recognised Barbadian educational institution
- Completed application forms
- Valid passport
- Proof of financial support (able to pay tuition and living expenses)
- Health insurance
- Evidence of accommodation

Your school or university will usually help guide you through the process and may assist with submitting documents.

WORKING WHILE STUDYING: Student visa holders are generally not permitted to work in Barbados without separate authorization from the Immigration Department.

CONTACT: Immigration.studentvisas@barbados.gov.bb | Tel: 535-4187, 4694, 4171, 4134, 4676, 4182

SECTION 7: EXTENDING YOUR STAY IN BARBADOS

If you entered Barbados as a visitor and want to stay longer than your original permitted period, you must apply for an Extension of Stay before your current permission expires.

You cannot simply overstay — this is a violation of immigration law and can result in deportation and a ban on future entry.

HOW TO APPLY FOR AN EXTENSION:
1. Visit the Immigration Department Head Office before your current stay expires
2. Complete Form B: "Application Under Section 13(3) of the Act for Permission to Vary Period of Stay in Barbados or Conditions of Stay"
3. Pay the required fee
4. Provide a reason for the extension (medical, family, or other valid grounds)

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- Extensions are not guaranteed — they are granted at the discretion of the Immigration Department
- You should apply well before your current permission expires, not on the last day
- Bring your passport, evidence of sufficient funds, and your accommodation details
- If your stay was extended for medical reasons, medical documentation may be required

CONTACT: Immigration.extensions@barbados.gov.bb | Tel: 535-4142, 4141, 4138

SECTION 8: LONG-TERM PATHWAYS — IMMIGRANT STATUS, PERMANENT RESIDENCE, AND CITIZENSHIP

For those looking to make Barbados their permanent home, there are formal pathways:

IMMIGRANT STATUS:
The first step toward long-term legal residency. You apply using Form A (Application for Immigrant Status and Non-National Registration). This grants you the right to reside in Barbados on an ongoing basis under certain conditions.

PERMANENT RESIDENT STATUS:
A more settled status that gives you the right to live and work in Barbados indefinitely. This typically requires a period of time spent legally in Barbados and meeting financial and character requirements.

CITIZENSHIP:
Barbadian citizenship can be obtained in several ways:
- By birth in Barbados (with certain conditions relating to parents' status)
- By descent (if a parent is Barbadian)
- By registration after marriage to a Barbadian citizen
- By naturalisation after residing legally in Barbados for a qualifying period
- Under specific constitutional provisions

General citizenship application requirements include:
- Proof of legal residence in Barbados for the required period
- Birth certificate and passport
- Police certificate of good character
- Marriage certificate (for spousal applications)
- Application fee
- Supporting documents specific to the pathway being used

The Immigration Department handles all citizenship applications. The Citizenship Section can be reached at Immigration.citizenship@barbados.gov.bb | Tel: 535-4164, 4677, 4155, 4130.

SECTION 9: FEES AND PAYMENTS — WHAT TO EXPECT

Barbados uses the Barbadian Dollar (BDS$). The BDS dollar is pegged to the US dollar at approximately BDS$2 = US$1 (or more precisely, BDS$1.98 = US$1).

COMMON APPLICATION FEES (as published by the Immigration Department):
- Entry visa application: BDS$300 (plus BDS$300 if approved)
- Work permit application: BDS$300 (final fee varies by category)
- Adult Barbados passport: BDS$150
- Child Barbados passport (under 16): BDS$100
- Urgent passport (1 day): BDS$300
- Urgent passport (2–5 days): BDS$225
- Replacement of lost passport: BDS$300
- Businessman's passport: BDS$225

Welcome Stamp: US$2,000 (individual) / US$3,000 (family) — paid in US dollars

HOW TO PAY:
- Many fees can be paid online through the Immigration Department portal at immigration.gov.bb before your appointment
- Print two copies of your payment receipt and bring them to your appointment
- Applications will not be accepted without payment of fees
- Overseas applicants can also access the online payment system

APPOINTMENT SYSTEM:
The Immigration Department operates an appointment system. You book online and pay the application fee during the booking process. On the appointment date, bring your payment receipt plus all required documents. Incomplete applications cause delays.
